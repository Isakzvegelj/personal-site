Paris 2024 — Reflections on the Olympic regatta

1 August 2026

Standing on the pontoon at Vaires-sur-Marne, waiting to push off for my first race at the Paris 2024 Olympics, I tried to take it all in. This was the moment years of training had pointed toward — representing Slovenia in the men's single sculls on the world's biggest stage.

Rowing a single scull is a strange kind of solitude. There is nobody to hide behind. Every stroke belongs to you, and so does every moment of doubt. At an Olympic Games that honesty is amplified. You feel the whole weight of the moment, and you learn, quickly, that the only thing you control is what you choose to do with the next stroke.

The preparation was most of the story. The early mornings that were grey and cold. The sessions when nothing felt right, and the sessions when the boat just sang. The people — coaches, family, teammates — who believed in me long before I had results to show for it. Rowing doesn't reward talent alone; it rewards persistence, and persistence is always a team effort even in a one-man boat.

Race week itself moved fast. Too fast, in some ways. But I remember holding close to the things that mattered: the course, the village, the strange quiet of the warm-up before each start. I kept reminding myself why I was there — not to prove anything to anyone else, but because I had earned the right to race among the best in the world.

The result is now history. What stays with me is the experience — what it took to get there, and what it taught me. The lessons carry into whatever comes next, on the water or off it: show up, trust the work, and row the boat you're in.

Paris gave me something I can't fully put into words, and I'm grateful for everyone who helped me get there.
