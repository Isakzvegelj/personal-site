A practical website checklist for hotels and hospitality businesses

1 November 2026

A hospitality website should help a guest decide whether a place fits their stay and make the next step clear. My portfolio includes hotel, accommodation and breakfast websites in Bled. Here is a practical checklist to use when planning a new site or reviewing an existing one.

Make the essentials easy to find

Give guests the location, the type of stay or meal, what is included and the next step. Use photographs that represent the actual property or business. Keep seasonal details, opening hours and contact information current.

Design the journey for a phone

Check the site on a small screen. Guests should be able to read room information, browse photographs and find a booking or enquiry action without fighting tiny controls. Use appropriately sized images so a visitor does not have to download desktop-sized photographs for every thumbnail.

Connect booking and enquiries clearly

A hotel may need live availability and a booking provider; a restaurant may need a table enquiry. Explain what happens when a guest taps the button. If the action opens an email app, say so. Test the handoff without placing an unintended booking.

Give each page a clear purpose

Write useful page titles and descriptions. Keep important information in readable HTML, link related pages, and maintain the sitemap when content changes. Search visibility depends on useful information and whether people can discover it; a checklist cannot promise rankings or bookings.

Measure enquiries and confirmed bookings

Search impressions, clicks, enquiries and confirmed bookings are different measures. Use Search Console to understand discovery and your actual booking or business records to understand outcomes. Compare equivalent periods and account for seasonality.

Explore live examples
